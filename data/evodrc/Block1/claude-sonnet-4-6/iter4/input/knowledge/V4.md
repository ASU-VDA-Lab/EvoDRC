## V4 Repair Knowledge (iteration 3)

### Rule summary and measured repair outcomes

**V4.W.1** (minimum width 24 nm along M5 length) and **V4.M4.EN.1 / V4.M5.EN.2** (11 nm enclosure on two opposite sides by M4 and M5, respectively) share the same geometric sensitivity: the via shape must be wide enough to satisfy the width rule while remaining enclosed by both metal layers. Trial trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA45_1_2_58_58.01 resolved 52 violations across two windows (unit:leaf_0034 and unit:leaf_0035) using a single `resize_via_shape` operation on M5, y-axis, -88 dbu on cell VIA_VIA45_1_2_58_58. The operation was applied via the cu_pool channel and connectivity was preserved. Shrinking the M5 shape along y in the cu_pool channel is therefore a directly confirmed, connection-safe strategy for eliminating violations in the locus [1728, 2068, 14256, 14132].

**V4.AUX.1** (V4 must lie inside both M4 and M5) is at risk whenever instance moves shift metal polygons without a corresponding adjustment to the via shape. Trial trial:i02.ug.leaf_0002.01 combined a [-76, 0] dbu instance move for i0181 with a +44 dbu `resize_end` on p1154 (y-axis, low end) and touched M4, M5, and V4; the gate check showed zero new in-crop and zero new out-of-crop violations, confirming that this paired move-and-resize approach kept V4 safely inside both enclosing metals. Trial trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 further confirms this: a [+76, 0] dbu move of i0181 paired with a -32 dbu `resize_end` on p1187 (x-axis, high end), also touching M4, M5, and V4 in locus [4020, 14548, 4320, 15936], produced zero new in-crop and zero new out-of-crop violations with connectivity preserved. Together these two trials establish that paired move-and-resize is safe for V4 containment on both the x-axis and y-axis.

**V4.M5.AUX.2** (V4 must match M5 width in the direction perpendicular to M5 length) is the rule most vulnerable to large-batch instance moves with a y-component. Trial trial:i02.ug.leaf_0003.02 applied 85 ops--predominantly x-axis instance moves of +32 or -16 dbu, with several ops that also carried a y-component (e.g., [32, -64], [32, -112], [-16, -64], [-16, -112], [32, -96], [-16, -96])--and introduced 4 new V4.M5.AUX.2 violations while touching M2, M3, M4, M5, M6, V2, V3, V4, and V5. No other V4 rule appeared in the new_in_crop breakdown for that trial, isolating V4.M5.AUX.2 as the specific exposure created by moves that laterally shift M5 polygons relative to V4 without a compensating via resize. The decision was still `gated_in` because connectivity was preserved and no new out-of-crop violations were produced, but the 4 new V4.M5.AUX.2 markers represent residual debt that must be addressed in subsequent iterations.

By contrast, trial trial:i02.ug.leaf_0004.03 applied 41 ops with the same mix of x-axis and compound x+y instance moves on an overlapping set of instances (several dropped from leaf_0003 due to `external_conflict_dropped` or `external_duplicate`) and produced zero new V4 violations of any kind, despite also touching M4, M5, and V4. The difference from trial:i02.ug.leaf_0003.02 is that many of the higher-magnitude y-component moves (e.g., [32, 72], [32, 24], [-16, 72], [-16, 24], [-16, -72], [32, -72]) that leaf_0003 executed were dropped from leaf_0004 via cross_crop_first_wins or external_conflict_dropped, indicating those y-displacements were the proximate cause of the V4.M5.AUX.2 introductions. Trial trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 is consistent with this: the instance move carried zero y-component ([76, 0]) and produced zero new V4.M5.AUX.2 violations.

### Operational guidance grounded in measured records

Do not apply large-batch instance moves with a y-component without verifying that M5 polygons interacting with V4 remain width-matched to those vias. Trial trial:i02.ug.leaf_0003.02 demonstrates that a set of moves otherwise safe for M1-M6 and other via layers can selectively introduce V4.M5.AUX.2 violations when M5 shifts transversely relative to the via. Pure x-component moves do not carry this risk: trial:i02.ug.leaf_0004.03 (x-only moves that survived conflict drops) and trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 (single x-only paired move) both produced zero V4.M5.AUX.2 violations.

When the cu_pool channel targets a via cell directly (as in trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA45_1_2_58_58.01), prefer `resize_via_shape` on the M5 layer along y before considering instance-level moves; the single-op, single-axis shrink resolved 52 violations without introducing any new violations.

For unit_gate moves touching M4/M5/V4, pair every instance move with a `resize_end` on the affected metal polygon endpoint. Trial trial:i02.ug.leaf_0002.01 (y-axis resize, y-axis move delta) and trial trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 (x-axis resize on p1187 high end, x-axis move delta) both confirm a zero-violation gate result under this discipline. The axis of the resize must correspond to the axis of the move displacement so that the metal endpoint tracks the instance shift without leaving V4 exposed.

**V4.S.1, V4.S.2, V4.S.3** (33 nm spacing, same-net and different-net, euclidean and projection) did not appear in any violation delta across any of the three unit_gate trials, the cu_pool trial, or trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00. No spacing-related V4 repair guidance can be drawn from the current history.

**GEOMETRY.NONORTHOGONAL** did not appear in any trial result for V4. All recorded ops (resize_via_shape, move_instance, resize_end) produced orthogonal geometry changes; no non-orthogonal edges were introduced.

Trial trial:i02.ug.leaf_0004.03 shows that ops claimed by both leaf_0003 and leaf_0004 on the same instances are resolved by `external_conflict_dropped` or `external_duplicate` in favor of the leaf that committed first (leaf_0003). This confirms that instance-move conflicts are resolved by first-writer priority and that the remainder set, when it excludes the high-y-delta moves, does not re-introduce V4.M5.AUX.2. Trial trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 recorded no conflict drops, consistent with its small op count (2 ops) and a locus ([4020, 14548, 4320, 15936]) distinct from the leaf_0003/leaf_0004 windows.
