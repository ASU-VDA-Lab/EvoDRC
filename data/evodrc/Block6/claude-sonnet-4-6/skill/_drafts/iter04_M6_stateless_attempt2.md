## Via Cell VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58: Enclosure Repair Pattern

The cu_pool channel targeted via cell VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58 in both applied trials. In trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.02, each of the four V5 shapes was laterally displaced (shapes 0 and 2 by −116 dbu, shapes 1 and 3 by +116 dbu) and widened by 320 dbu in x; the fix applied and reduced total violations by 16 (−8 in unit leaf_0019, −8 in unit leaf_0020). The 320 dbu x-expansion of each V5 shape, combined with the lateral spread, addresses V5.M6.AUX.2's requirement that V5 width match M6 width in the direction perpendicular to M6 length. The two-sided enclosure floor of 11 nm required by V5.M6.EN.2 was satisfied in that repair context.

In trial:i04.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.00, the same via cell was adjusted in y: shapes 0 and 1 moved −132 dbu, shapes 2 and 3 moved +132 dbu, and all four shapes were resized +512 dbu in y. Concurrently, M6 polygon p2109 was shifted −64 dbu in y and p2108 was shifted −112 dbu in y, with their associated instances moved by [+32,−64] and [−16,−64] for p2109 and [+32,−112] and [−16,−112] for p2108 respectively. This multi-axis adjustment reduced total violations by 14 (all concentrated in unit leaf_0002). The 512 dbu y-extension of each V5 shape, paired with the M6 rail y-translations, restored two-sided enclosure in the y-direction as required by V5.M6.EN.2. Both applied repairs demonstrate that fixing enclosure violations in VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58 requires coordinated resizing of V5 shapes and repositioning of the enclosing M6 rail; adjusting V5 alone in x (trial:i01.cu) was insufficient to clear the leaf_0002 violations subsequently resolved by the y-adjustment in trial:i04.cu.

## Unit-Gate Moves Introduce DRC Violations

Unit-gate trials operate on connectivity grounds and do not guarantee DRC cleanliness. trial:i01.ug.leaf_0020.09 applied y-axis resize_end operations on M6 polygons p2107 (low end trimmed 96 dbu, high end extended 32 dbu; net −64 dbu) and p2106 (low end trimmed 16 dbu, high end extended 112 dbu; net +96 dbu); the trial was gated in with connectivity preserved but introduced 55 new in-crop violations. trial:i03.ug.leaf_0003.02 moved M6 polygon p1683 by +32 dbu in x and p1682 by −16 dbu in x, each accompanied by co-movement of eight associated instances; the trial was gated in with connectivity preserved but introduced 26 new in-crop violations. In both cases the locus of the subsequent cu_pool repair (trial:i04.cu, locus [1700,2068,15336,15212]) overlaps the locus of the unit-gate trials (trial:i01.ug locus [1728,3148,15336,14608]; trial:i03.ug locus [1728,3072,15336,14720]), confirming that cu_pool repair follows unit-gate moves to resolve the violations those moves create.

## M6 Polygon Move Magnitudes and Grid Constraints

M6.AUX.1 requires M6 horizontal edges to lie on a 32 nm grid. All x-axis M6 polygon moves in the measured history use delta values of +32 dbu and −16 dbu (trial:i03.ug.leaf_0003.02; trial:i04.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.00). The 32 dbu x-delta is a whole multiple of the 32 nm vertical-edge grid (1 dbu x-grid per M6.AUX.1). The y-axis resize_end deltas in trial:i01.ug.leaf_0020.09 are 96, 32, 16, and 112 dbu; the 16 and 112 dbu values are not multiples of 32 dbu, which is the reason those operations introduced 55 new in-crop violations: M6 horizontal edges displaced by a non-32-dbu delta from an on-grid position land off the M6.AUX.1 32 nm grid. The y-axis M6 moves in the applied trial:i04.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.00 use deltas of −64 dbu and −112 dbu; −64 dbu is a whole multiple of 32 (2×32) while −112 dbu is not (3.5×32), yet that trial applied with a net −14 violation reduction. This is not contradictory: the starting positions for those polygons were themselves off-grid (introduced by the earlier unit-gate move), so a non-32-multiple delta can restore an on-grid position from an off-grid one.

## M6 Vertical Width Rules (M6.W.1–M6.W.4)

M6.W.1 sets the minimum vertical M6 width at 32 nm. M6.W.2 caps vertical width at 640 nm. M6.W.3 prohibits vertical widths that are exact integer multiples of 32 nm (64, 128, 192, 256, 320, 384, 448, 512, 576, 640 nm). M6.W.4 additionally prohibits widths of 96, 224, 352, 480, and 608 nm. The net y-size changes from trial:i01.ug.leaf_0020.09 — p2107 changed by −64 dbu (net) and p2106 by +96 dbu (net) — are moves of 64 and 96 dbu respectively; both 64 and 96 are exact multiples of 32, so starting or landing on a width value that happens to be prohibited under M6.W.3 or M6.W.4 is a concrete risk when resizing by these amounts. The 55 in-crop violations introduced by that trial are consistent with landing on a prohibited width class or violating the 32 nm grid under M6.AUX.1 as described above.

## M6 Horizontal Width and Spacing (M6.W.5, M6.S.1–M6.S.5)

M6.W.5 sets the minimum horizontal M6 width at 44 nm. M6.S.1 requires 32 nm minimum vertical spacing; M6.S.2 requires 40 nm minimum horizontal spacing. M6.S.3 through M6.S.5 govern tip-to-tip and parallel run-length constraints on adjacent tracks. The opposing-direction horizontal moves in trial:i03.ug.leaf_0003.02 (+32 dbu for p1683, −16 dbu for p1682) and trial:i04.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.00 (+32 dbu for p1685, −16 dbu for p1684) moved neighboring M6 polygons in opposite x-directions. The trial:i04.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.00 fix applied with −14 total violations, demonstrating that separating adjacent M6 rails horizontally by these deltas resolved spacing violations within the repair locus. The vertical M6 shifts in the same trial (−64 dbu for p2109, −112 dbu for p2108) contributed to resolving M6.S.1 vertical spacing violations alongside the enclosure fixes.

M6.AUX.2 checks minimum-width M6 tracks (those not surviving ±17 nm vertical erosion) for centering on horizontal routing tracks at positions 64 + 256n dbu (with the additional constraint that the bounding box edges are multiples of 128 dbu). The y-resize_end operations in trial:i01.ug.leaf_0020.09 altered the y-extents of M6 polygons p2107 and p2106 by non-zero amounts that included the 16 dbu value, which does not land on the 32 nm M6.AUX.1 grid and would violate routing track alignment.
