## Effective Operation Types

**`move_instance`** in the +x direction is the primary repair mechanism used across all iterations. Positive x-axis translations ranging from 4 dbu (trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row7.01) to 112 dbu (trial:i01.ug.leaf_0001.04) were accepted without introducing new M1.W.1, M1.S.1, M1.S.2, M1.S.3, M1.S.4, M1.S.5, M1.S.6, V0.M1.EN.1, V0.M1.AUX.3, or V1.M1.EN.1 violations. Do not avoid small delta moves: a translation of 4 dbu resolved the remaining spacing gap after the coarser +104 dbu first-pass move on the same unit (trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row7.01 following trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02), demonstrating that fine-tuning steps after a larger initial move are productive.

One instance of a negative x-axis move (delta_dbu: [-36, 0]) was applied to inst i0074 in trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02 within the same locus as a +104 dbu move on a different instance. The combined result was accepted without new violations. Do not treat negative-direction moves as forbidden: the gated-in outcome in trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02 confirms that counter-directional instance adjustments within a multi-instance operation can satisfy M1 spacing and enclosure rules simultaneously.

**`resize_end`** on M1 polygons along the x-axis with `end: high` (extending the right edge) was used in trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row5.01 (delta_dbu: 92, polygon p2072), trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02 (delta_dbu: 124, polygon p1903), trial:i01.ug.leaf_0001.04 (delta_dbu: 132, polygon p2016), trial:i01.ug.leaf_0015.06 (delta_dbu: 48, polygon p1923), and trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row4.00 (delta_dbu: 132, polygon p2020). All were accepted. Use `resize_end axis:x end:high` to extend M1 polygon tips when enclosure of V0 (V0.M1.EN.1) or V1 (V1.M1.EN.1) is insufficient on the high-x side, or when M1.A.1 area requirements are not met after an instance move.

One `resize_end` with `end: low` was applied to polygon p1920 (delta_dbu: 56) in trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02, also accepted. Apply `end: low` to extend the low-x edge when the enclosure deficit or tip clearance violation is on the left side of the polygon.

## Multi-Instance Coordinated Moves

Multiple instances within the same locus can be moved simultaneously. Trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row3.00 moved 3 instances (i0471, i0324, i0481) each by +72 dbu. Trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row5.01 moved 4 instances by +36 dbu and also applied a resize. Trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02 combined 2 moves (one +104, one -36) with 2 resize_end operations. Trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row4.00 moved 2 instances by different deltas (+56 and +112) alongside a resize. All were accepted. When M1 violations span multiple instances within a locus, move all contributing instances together in the same operation; split moves across separate trials only when the required delta differs significantly between units (as in trial:i02.ug.leaf_0004.04 which isolated instance i0446 that was moved +72 dbu separately from the cluster in trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row5.01).

## Iteration Convergence Pattern

The same unit can require multiple iterations. `Block6_union_row7` received repair in iter 1 (trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02, n_ops: 4) and again in iter 2 (trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row7.01, n_ops: 1, delta: +4 dbu). `Block6_union_row8` received repair in iter 1 (trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row8.03, n_ops: 1) and iter 2 (trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row8.02, n_ops: 2). `leaf_0001` received repair in iter 1 (trial:i01.ug.leaf_0001.04) and iter 3 (trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00). Apply small corrective moves in subsequent iterations for units that were repaired with large deltas in earlier iterations: the large move resolves the primary violation but can create a secondary residual gap that requires a follow-up fine-tuning step (as demonstrated by the +104 / +4 dbu two-step sequence on Block6_union_row7 across trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02 and trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row7.01).

## Layer Co-modification

Every accepted trial touched layers M1, M2, and V1 together (`touched_layers: ["M1","M2","V1"]`). Do not modify M1 in isolation when repairing M1 spacing or enclosure violations: the accepted moves in trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row3.00 through trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 all propagated changes to V1 and M2 as part of the same operation, and all were gated in cleanly. Moving an instance displaces all its internal geometry simultaneously across all layers, which is the mechanism that keeps V1.M1.EN.1 and V0.M1.EN.1 enclosure relationships intact after the move.

## Orthogonality Preservation

All resize and move operations in the accepted trials produce only axis-aligned geometry. The NONORTHOGONAL rule fires on any edge with angle 1–89, 91–179, -179..-91, or -89..-1 degrees on M1. Every resize_end in the history is axis:x (either end:high or end:low), producing horizontal or vertical edge changes only. Do not use diagonal or angled resize or placement adjustments: all 13 accepted trials used strictly rectilinear deltas and produced no NONORTHOGONAL violations.

## V0 and V1 Enclosure Maintenance

Instance moves that co-translate V0, M1, and V1 together preserve enclosure rule compliance by keeping the relative positions of via and metal unchanged. Trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row3.00, trial:i01.ug.leaf_0011.05, trial:i01.ug.leaf_0018.07, and trial:i03.ug.leaf_0001.00 used single-instance moves without any resize, and all were accepted with no new V0.M1.EN.1, V0.M1.AUX.3, or V1.M1.EN.1 violations. When the repair target is a spacing violation between adjacent M1 segments (M1.S.1 through M1.S.6), prefer whole-instance translation over polygon-only resizing when the instance contains vias, because translation keeps V0.M1.AUX.3 (V0 width must match M1 width along perpendicular direction) trivially satisfied. Resize_end alone was always paired with at least one move_instance in the same trial (trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row5.01, trial:i01.ug.Block6_union_row7.02, trial:i01.ug.leaf_0001.04, trial:i01.ug.leaf_0015.06, trial:i02.ug.Block6_union_row4.00), confirming that standalone polygon extension without an accompanying instance adjustment was not used in any accepted trial.
