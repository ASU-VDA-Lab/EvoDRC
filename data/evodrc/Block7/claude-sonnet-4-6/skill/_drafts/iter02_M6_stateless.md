## Via-cell M6 geometry: avoid vertical expansion of M6 via shapes

Resizing M6 vertically inside a via cell definition produced a net increase of 234 violations and was rejected (trial:i02.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.01). That trial applied a +128 dbu y-axis resize to the M6 shape in cell VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58 alongside a −96 dbu x-axis resize of M5. The net-positive outcome means this class of M6 modification must be avoided as a repair strategy for this cell and analogous via cells in the same locus (1728, 2068, 28728, 28172).

The M6.W.3 rule forbids vertical widths that are even integer multiples of the 32 nm minimum: 64, 128, 192, 256, 320, 384, 448, 512, 576, and 640 nm are all illegal. A +128 dbu (128 nm) y-resize easily pushes an M6 shape into one of these forbidden widths or, combined with the original shape dimension, produces a result that lands on the M6.W.4 prohibited set (96, 224, 352, 480, 608 nm). Do not apply y-axis expansions to M6 via shapes without first computing the resulting absolute width and confirming it falls outside every value enumerated by both M6.W.3 and M6.W.4.

M6.AUX.1 requires all M6 horizontal edges to sit on a 32 nm grid. Any y-axis resize whose delta is not a multiple of 32 nm (in dbu units where 1 dbu = 1 nm) will shift an edge off-grid. The +128 dbu delta in trial:i02.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.01 is divisible by 32, so grid violation alone does not explain the rejection, but any resize whose delta is not a multiple of 32 will create M6.AUX.1 errors on top of any width violations.

## V5 reshaping inside via cells is the effective repair path for V5.M6.EN.2

Trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.02 was applied and reduced the total violation count by 80 across windows unit:Block7_union_row21, unit:Block7_union_row22, unit:leaf_0103, and unit:leaf_0104. That trial operated exclusively on V5 shapes within the same cell VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58: four pairs of (move_via_shape, resize_via_shape) operations along the y-axis, with moves of ±132 dbu and resizes of +512 dbu each. M6 was recorded as a touched layer but received no direct edits; the M6 violation count improved solely as a consequence of V5 repositioning.

V5.M6.EN.2 requires that V5 be enclosed by M6 by at least 11 nm on two opposite sides. When V5 shapes are shifted or sized incorrectly relative to the enclosing M6 geometry, this rule fires. The repair pattern confirmed by trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.02 is to move V5 shapes toward the M6 center (±132 dbu offsets) and expand them in y (+512 dbu) so that each V5 shape achieves the required bilateral 11 nm margin within the existing M6 boundary. This avoids touching M6 at all and bypasses the width and track-placement constraints that make direct M6 resizes hazardous.

V5.M6.AUX.2 additionally requires that V5 be exactly the same width as M6 in the direction perpendicular to M6's length (i.e., the same vertical extent when M6 runs horizontally). The +512 dbu resize applied to all four V5 shapes in trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.02 is consistent with bringing V5 into flush alignment with M6's vertical span; the symmetric ±132 dbu moves center the V5 shapes so that their edges coincide with M6 edges on both sides, satisfying the coincident-edge count check that V5.M6.AUX.2 performs.

## M6 track placement constraints limit which y-positions are legal after any resize

M6.AUX.2 requires that minimum-width M6 tracks have their centerlines on a 256 nm pitch grid with a 64 nm offset from the origin (for shapes whose bottom and top are both multiples of 128 nm). Any operation that shifts the vertical position of an M6 shape — whether by moving M6 itself or by resizing in a way that implicitly displaces centerlines — must land the resulting centerline on this grid. Trial:i02.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.01 resized M6 by +128 dbu in y; whether that displaced the centerline off-grid depends on the original shape coordinates, but the confirmed rejection (delta_total = +234) shows the resulting geometry was non-compliant.

M6.AUX.4 prohibits the horizontal outer edges of wide M6 shapes from coinciding with routing track edges occupied by separate minimum-width M6 wires. Wide shapes are those that survive the `m6.sized(0, -17.nm).sized(0, 17.nm)` erosion, meaning shapes with vertical width ≥ 34 nm on both sides. Expanding a borderline-wide M6 shape by 128 nm in y increases the risk of its outer edges aligning with a track edge of a nearby 1x M6 wire, triggering M6.AUX.4. This is a secondary mechanism consistent with the high rejection delta observed in trial:i02.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.01.

## Summary of repair priorities for M6 violations in via cells

Operate on V5 shapes (y-axis moves and resizes) to resolve V5.M6.EN.2 and V5.M6.AUX.2 without touching M6; trial:i01.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.02 confirms this reduces M6-window violations by 80. Do not resize M6 in y inside via cells: trial:i02.cu.def:VIA_VIA56_2_2_66_58.01 confirms that a +128 dbu y-resize of M6 in the same cell increases violations by 234. When a y-resize of M6 is unavoidable in other contexts, verify that the resulting absolute vertical width avoids all values in {64, 96, 128, 192, 224, 256, 320, 352, 384, 448, 480, 512, 576, 608, 640} nm and that all horizontal edges after the resize remain on the 32 nm grid.
