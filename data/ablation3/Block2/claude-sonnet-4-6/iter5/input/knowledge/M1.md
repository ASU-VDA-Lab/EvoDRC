## Observed Repair Patterns and Outcomes

**Co-moving M1 polygons with associated instances is safe when connectivity is preserved.**
In trial:i02.ug.whole_design.00, M1 polygons p937 and p938 were each translated +32 dbu in x, while a large set of cell instances (i0110, i0119, i0098, and others) were moved by the same or correlated x-displacements. The trial was accepted (gated_in) with zero new violations inside and outside the crop window. When a move of an M1 segment is matched by corresponding displacement of the vias it encloses and the instances that drive those vias, no new M1.W.1, M1.S.1, V0.M1.EN.1, or V1.M1.EN.1 violations result.

**Stretching the high-x end of M1 polygons while moving connected cell instances preserves enclosure rules.**
In trial:i02.ug.whole_design.00, eight M1 polygons (p958 through p965) each received a resize_end on the x-axis, high end, by +32 dbu, concurrent with instance moves by [32, 0] or equivalent coordinated y-offsets. None of these operations produced new violations. The approach of extending an M1 end to follow an instance being repositioned is verified to keep V0.M1.EN.1 (minimum 5 nm enclosure on two opposite sides) and V1.M1.EN.1 (5 nm and 2 nm opposite-side enclosure) intact, provided the resize amount matches the instance displacement.

**Uniform same-delta x-only instance moves paired with matching M1 end-stretches are a reliable and repeatable repair pattern.**
In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, 11 instances (i0086, i0018, i0159, i0027, i0152, i0103, i0127, i0115, i0034, i0083, i0132) were each moved [72, 0] dbu, and 11 M1 polygons (p1065, p1045, p1026, p1052, p1053, p1040, p1063, p1034, p1057, p1059, p1060) each received a +72 dbu resize_end on the x-axis high end. The result was gated_in with zero new violations. This confirms that applying a single uniform x-displacement simultaneously to a fleet of instances and their corresponding M1 end-stretches--with no y-offset--satisfies all M1 width, spacing, area, and enclosure constraints without additional y-axis edits.

**Larger end-stretches of a single M1 polygon are also viable when spacing budget allows, and iterative extension across multiple repair passes accumulates without violation.**
In trial:i02.ug.whole_design.00, polygon p1065 received a resize_end of +116 dbu on the x-axis high end, and p1036 received +80 dbu, both with zero new violations. In trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, p1036 received a further +84 dbu resize_end, accumulating +164 dbu total without triggering M1.W.1, M1.S.1, M1.S.2, M1.A.1, or enclosure violations. In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, p1065 received an additional +72 dbu resize_end, bringing its cumulative extension to +188 dbu across iterations 2 and 4, again with zero new violations. Iterative extension of the same polygon across consecutive repair iterations is safe when the surrounding spacing budget is not exhausted.

**Instance moves paired with M1 end-stretches must preserve both x and y enclosure simultaneously.**
In trial:i02.ug.whole_design.00, several instance groups were moved with y-offsets (e.g., [32, -48], [32, 48], [32, 72], [32, -72], [32, -96], [32, 96]) while the M1 polygons connecting them received only x-axis resize_end operations. The fact that no V0.M1.EN.1 or V0.M1.AUX.3 violations appeared confirms that the y-displacements remained within the existing M1 enclosure margins in the y-direction. V0.M1.AUX.3 requires V0 width to match M1 width along the perpendicular axis; this constraint was satisfied without additional M1 y-axis edits, indicating that the M1 polygons in this design already provided sufficient perpendicular enclosure margin to absorb the instance y-offsets applied. In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, all instance moves were pure x-only ([72, 0]), further confirming that x-axis end-stretches with no y-component require no y-axis M1 edits.

**Connectivity preservation is a reliable gating criterion for M1 repair acceptance.**
Trials trial:i02.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, and trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00 were all accepted with decision "gated_in" and reason "conn_preserved". In all three cases, n_new_in_crop and n_new_out_of_crop were zero. This establishes that operations on M1 that preserve net connectivity do not introduce new DRC violations in this design context, and the channel "unit_gate" consistently accepts such moves.
