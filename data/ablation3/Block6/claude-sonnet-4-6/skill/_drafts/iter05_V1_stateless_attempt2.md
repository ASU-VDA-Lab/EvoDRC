**V1.W.1 — Minimum width 18 nm along M2 length**

The rule requires each V1 instance to be at least 18 nm wide measured along the M2 track direction. In trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, the solver paired M2 resize_end extensions (e.g., +152 dbu and +116 dbu on the high-x ends of p2071, p2020, p1903, and others) with move_instance ops on the corresponding via cells rather than resizing V1 bodies directly; all changes were accepted with no new V1.W.1 violations. In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00 and trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00, the same pattern held: V1 widths were left intact while surrounding M1/M2 geometry was adjusted. Do not use axis-aligned resize on V1 polygons to fix enclosure or spacing violations when the resize would bring the via width below 18 nm; use move or move_instance instead, as demonstrated in trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, and trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00.

**V1.M2.EN.2 — M2 enclosure of V1, two opposite sides 5 & 5 nm or 5 & 0 nm**

M2 must enclose V1 by 5 nm on at least one projection axis on each of two opposite edges; the opposite member of the pair may be 0 nm (flush/coincident) but not negative. In trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, seven M2 polygons (p2071, p2020, p2072, p2086, p1946, p1903, p1991) received resize_end operations extending their high-x ends; each extension was paired with a move_instance that shifted the via cell in the same direction by the same displacement (96 dbu), preserving relative alignment while adding M2 material beyond the V1 edge. This pattern directly satisfies the rule by growing the enclosing M2 boundary away from a short-enclosure edge. Apply resize_end on the deficient M2 end combined with a matching move_instance on the via instance when projection enclosure is short; do not resize the V1 body, because shrinking V1 risks V1.W.1 as shown by the exclusively M2-side resize strategy confirmed across trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00 and trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00. The rule also fires when V1 is not inside M2 at all (caught by v1_en2_ep_zero); ensure move_instance displacements do not move a via cell outside its M2 boundary, a constraint respected in every accepted trial (trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00).

**V1.M1.EN.1 — M1 enclosure of V1, two opposite sides 5 & 2 nm**

M1 must enclose V1 by 5 nm on one projection axis and at least 2 nm on the opposing axis of the same direction pair; a via failing both thresholds on any axis triggers the rule. All three accepted iterations touched M1 and V1 together (trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00), and all were accepted, confirming that move_instance displacements applied to via cells were bounded by the available M1 enclosure on both axes. When a move_instance shifts a via, verify the tighter 2 nm side first, as it is the binding constraint that fails before the 5 nm side when displacement is large; trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00 used +96 dbu instance moves paired with M2 resize_end, keeping V1 within M1 bounds without any M1 edits.

**V1.S.1 — Minimum spacing between V1 instances (18 nm same-track, 27 nm parallel not-aligned, 18 nm parallel aligned)**

The V1.S.1 checker operates on derived masks (v1_mask) that extend V1 geometry to include M2 end-cap regions; spacing is measured by projection on vertical edges. In trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00, resize_end ops on three M2 polygons (p1831, p1786, p1806) applied −20 dbu on the high-x end, shrinking M2 material; the corresponding instance groups were simultaneously moved by −12 dbu. Shrinking the M2 end-cap reduces the effective v1_mask extent, directly relaxing V1.S.1 spacing measured through that mask. Use resize_end with a negative delta on the M2 end that faces a spacing-violating neighbor, paired with a move of the via group, to increase the inter-mask gap; trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00 confirmed this approach resolves same-track or parallel-track spacing violations without creating V1.W.1 or V1.M2.EN.2 regressions.

**V1.S.2 — Minimum corner-to-corner spacing 23 nm (both vias with 5 nm M2 end-cap, Euclidean)**

V1.S.2 uses Euclidean distance on the v1_wec_mask (the mask for vias whose M2 edges are not fully coincident with M2 boundary). The 16.4 nm Euclidean check on the mask corresponds to 23 nm corner-to-corner distance at the actual via corners. In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, symmetric resize ops (+10 dbu on axis x for p1990, p1923, p1920) and move_instance adjustments in multiple directions were accepted, indicating that small symmetric M2 resizes combined with lateral instance moves can reposition the wec_mask without triggering V1.S.2. Avoid move_instance displacements that bring two wec-category vias diagonally close when both carry a 5 nm end-cap; the diagonal component of the displacement accumulates in Euclidean distance faster than in projection, as the rule uses euclidean metric exclusively for this category (confirmed by accepted geometry in trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00 and trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00).

**V1.S.3 — Minimum corner-to-corner spacing 30 nm (both vias without 5 nm M2 end-cap, Euclidean, non-projection violations only)**

V1.S.3 fires only on Euclidean violations that are not also projection violations (v1_s3_all minus v1_s3_proj), targeting true corner-to-corner near-misses between nec-category vias. The 30 nm minimum is larger than any of the V1.S.1 projection thresholds, making diagonal placement the risk scenario. In trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00, multiple via groups were moved by +37 dbu or +13 dbu along x while M2 polygons moved by equal amounts, keeping V1 co-located with its M2 and preserving inter-via diagonal distance. Move via instances only along the M2 track direction (x in the observed trials) to avoid introducing diagonal proximity; trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, and trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00 all used exclusively axis-aligned displacements on V1-touching instances.

**V1.S.4 — Minimum corner-to-corner spacing 27 nm (mixed wec/nec pair, Euclidean, non-projection violations only)**

V1.S.4 is the cross-category analog of V1.S.3, measuring Euclidean separation between the wec_mask of one via and the nec_mask of another. The 17.11 nm Euclidean threshold on masks corresponds to 27 nm at actual via corners. Because wec and nec masks are constructed differently (wec_mask is sized +5/−5 nm; nec_mask includes the extended end-cap), their effective extents differ, and a purely axial displacement that resolves a V1.S.1 violation can simultaneously shift a wec via diagonally closer to a nec neighbor. In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, the −96 dbu moves on i0112 and i0152 alongside +96 dbu moves on i0319 and i0213 reflect counter-directed adjustments that resolved spacing on one axis without creating new diagonal violations; the trial was accepted, confirming the net separation was adequate. When adjusting a mixed wec/nec pair, apply opposite-direction moves that increase both axial and diagonal separation simultaneously rather than moving only one via, as demonstrated in trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00.

**V1.AUX.1 — V1 must be inside M1 ∩ M2**

Every V1 instance must lie fully within the intersection of M1 and M2. In trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, and trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00, all move_instance ops on via cells were paired with corresponding moves or resizes of the enclosing M2 polygon (same polygon_id pattern, same delta), ensuring V1 never moved outside M2. Apply the same delta to the via instance and its host M2 polygon whenever moving a V1 cell; the consistent pairing across all accepted trials prevents V1.AUX.1 from firing due to a via cell migrating beyond its M2 boundary. Never move a via instance independently of its enclosing M2 without first verifying that the new position still lies within both M1 and M2 extents, as the absence of any standalone via-only moves in trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, and trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00 demonstrates this was the enforced discipline.

**V1.M2.AUX.2 — V1 width must exactly match M2 width perpendicular to M2 length**

V1 must not extend beyond M2 transversely, and M2 must not be wider than V1 in that direction (the rule requires exactly two coincident edges). In trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, three M2 polygons (p1990, p1923, p1920) received symmetric resize ops (+10 dbu on axis x); the symmetric op ("resize", not "resize_end") grows both sides equally, which maintains the transverse coincidence condition when M2 and V1 are already co-extensive in that axis and the resize is along the orthogonal (length) axis. Prefer symmetric resize over asymmetric resize_end when adjusting M2 along its length direction so that the transverse edges of M2 remain coincident with V1; trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00 confirmed that symmetric M2 length resizes do not generate V1.M2.AUX.2 violations. Do not use resize_end to widen M2 transversely past the V1 edge, as that would break the coincidence requirement; the exclusively longitudinal resize strategy observed in trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00 and trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00 avoids this failure mode.

**GEOMETRY.NONORTHOGONAL — All V1 edges must be 0° or 90°**

The nonorthogonal check fires on any V1 edge deviating from 0° or 90°. All operations applied to V1 in every accepted trial were strictly axis-aligned: move and move_instance ops specify delta_dbu as [x, 0] (x-only) or resize_end ops specify axis:"x" with integer dbu deltas (trial:i03.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i04.ug.whole_design.00, trial:i05.ug.whole_design.00). No rotation, shear, or diagonal displacement appears for any V1-touching polygon across all three trials. Apply only axis-aligned move and resize operations to V1 polygons and their host instances; the complete absence of non-axis-aligned ops in all accepted trials confirms this is the necessary repair discipline to avoid GEOMETRY.NONORTHOGONAL violations on V1.
